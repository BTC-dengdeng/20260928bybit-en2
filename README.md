<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">1. What Is Isolated Margin on Bybit?</h2>

<p><strong>Isolated Margin manages the margin assigned to each position separately.</strong></p>

<p>For example, suppose you hold:</p>

<ul>
<li>A BTCUSDT long position;</li>
<li>An ETHUSDT long position.</li>
</ul>

<p>Under Isolated Margin, the margin risk of the BTC position and the ETH position is calculated separately.</p>

<p>If BTC moves sharply against your position and the BTC Mark Price reaches the position's Liquidation Price, the BTC position may be liquidated. However, <strong>the system generally does not directly use the isolated margin allocated to the ETH position to keep the BTC position alive</strong>.</p>

<p>The main characteristic of Isolated Margin is therefore:</p>

<p><strong>It limits the margin risk of a single position to a relatively isolated pool of funds.</strong></p>

<p>The trade-off is lower capital efficiency. Margin already allocated to one isolated position cannot be shared as freely with other positions as it can under Cross Margin.</p>

<hr style="border:none;border-top:1px solid #ddd;margin:36px 0">

<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">2. What Is Cross Margin on Bybit?</h2>

<p><strong>Cross Margin allows eligible available margin in the Unified Trading Account to support multiple positions and orders together.</strong></p>

<p>For example, if you hold BTC, ETH and SOL derivatives positions at the same time, and the BTC position develops a large unrealized loss, the account may continue using shared available margin to support that position as long as sufficient margin remains.</p>

<p>This means Cross Margin can:</p>

<ul>
<li>Improve capital efficiency;</li>
<li>Allow gains and losses across eligible derivatives positions to affect one another;</li>
<li>Use more available account assets to support overall account risk.</li>
</ul>

<p>However, it also creates an important risk:</p>

<p><strong>A position that keeps losing may gradually consume available margin across the entire UTA and eventually affect other positions.</strong></p>

<p>Therefore, seeing a liquidation price that appears farther away under Cross Margin does not mean the structure is automatically safer. The risk has simply shifted from the level of one position to the level of the entire account.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">4. When Is an Isolated Margin Position Liquidated?</h2>

<p>Under Bybit Isolated Margin, each position has its own allocated margin and Liquidation Price.</p>

<p><strong>When the Mark Price reaches the position's Liquidation Price, the position may enter liquidation.</strong></p>

<p>One important detail is:</p>

<p><strong>Bybit uses Mark Price as an important liquidation trigger, rather than simply using the Last Traded Price shown on the chart.</strong></p>

<p>This means the following situation can occur:</p>

<ul>
<li>The latest traded price on the chart appears not to have reached the liquidation level;</li>
<li>But the Mark Price has already reached the Liquidation Price;</li>
<li>The position can still be liquidated.</li>
</ul>

<p>If your stop-loss uses Last Traded Price as the trigger source and the stop price is too close to the Liquidation Price, Mark Price may trigger liquidation before the stop-loss order is activated.</p>

<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">5. When Is a Cross Margin Account Liquidated?</h2>

<p>The key difference with Cross Margin is that <strong>liquidation is not determined only by a fixed liquidation price for one individual position</strong>.</p>

<p>Under Bybit UTA Cross Margin, the system continuously calculates account-level metrics such as:</p>

<ul>
<li>Account Equity;</li>
<li>Available Margin;</li>
<li>Initial Margin Rate (IMR);</li>
<li>Maintenance Margin Rate (MMR);</li>
<li>Unrealized P&amp;L across relevant positions.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>When the Account Maintenance Margin Rate (MMR) reaches 100%, the account enters liquidation conditions.</strong></p>

<p>Therefore, although Bybit may display a Liquidation Price under Cross Margin, this value should mainly be treated as a <strong>reference</strong>.</p>

<p>It may change as:</p>

<ul>
<li>Account Equity changes;</li>
<li>Other positions gain or lose value;</li>
<li>Positions are opened or closed;</li>
<li>Available margin changes;</li>
<li>Maintenance Margin requirements change.</li>
</ul>

<p>For this reason, watching only one “Liquidation Price” is not enough under Cross Margin. <strong>Account MMR is one of the most important indicators of account-level liquidation risk.</strong></p>

<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">6. Why Can Cross Margin Show a More Distant Liquidation Price but Still Carry Significant Risk?</h2>

<p>Suppose you open a BTC long position.</p>

<p>Under Isolated Margin, you allocate only 1,000 USDT as the margin available to that BTC position. The position's risk is therefore primarily calculated using that isolated margin.</p>

<p>If you switch to Cross Margin and your UTA has another 5,000 USDT available as margin, the BTC position may be supported by a larger shared pool of funds.</p>

<p>The result may be:</p>

<p><strong>The displayed Liquidation Price moves farther away.</strong></p>

<p>But there is another side to this:</p>

<p><strong>If BTC continues moving against the position, the trade may continue consuming account margin that you did not originally intend to allocate to the BTC position.</strong></p>

<p>So the difference should not be understood as:</p>

<p>“Isolated is dangerous, Cross is safe.”</p>

<p>A more accurate description is:</p>

<p><strong>Isolated Margin concentrates the risk within a specific position, while Cross Margin allows that risk to spread into the shared account margin pool.</strong></p>

<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">7. What Is the Difference Between Initial Margin and Maintenance Margin?</h2>

<p>Before comparing Isolated and Cross Margin, it is useful to understand two key concepts.</p>

<h3 style="font-size:23px;line-height:1.6">Initial Margin (IM)</h3>

<p><strong>Initial Margin is the amount of margin required to establish a position.</strong></p>

<p>A larger position or lower leverage generally requires more initial margin.</p>

<h3 style="font-size:23px;line-height:1.6">Maintenance Margin (MM)</h3>

<p><strong>Maintenance Margin is the minimum amount of margin required to keep a position open.</strong></p>

<p>As the market moves against your position, unrealized losses reduce your remaining margin buffer.</p>

<p>If risk continues to increase:</p>

<ul>
<li>Under Isolated Margin, liquidation may occur when Mark Price reaches the position's Liquidation Price;</li>
<li>Under Cross Margin, liquidation is primarily determined by whether the account MMR reaches 100%.</li>
</ul>

<p>In Bybit's current derivatives margin framework, some IM and MM calculations also use Mark Price, so margin risk should not be understood as being determined only by the original entry price.</p>

<hr style="border:none;border-top:1px solid #ddd;margin:36px 0">

<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">8. Does Higher Leverage Always Mean Higher Liquidation Risk?</h2>

<p>Leverage directly affects initial margin requirements and the amount of margin buffer available to a position.</p>

<p>If other conditions are equal, higher leverage generally means:</p>

<ul>
<li>Less initial margin is required for the same notional position size;</li>
<li>The position may have less room to absorb adverse price movement;</li>
<li>The position may be closer to liquidation conditions.</li>
</ul>

<p>However, one important distinction is:</p>

<p><strong>The actual amount of trading profit or loss is primarily determined by the position's notional size and the price movement, not by the leverage number itself.</strong></p>

<p>For example, if two traders both hold a BTC long position with a notional value of 10,000 USDT and BTC falls by 5%, their price-based position loss will be broadly similar before fees and other factors.</p>

<p>The main difference is how much margin each trader committed and how large that loss is relative to the margin supporting the position.</p>

<div style="border:1px solid #ddd;border-left:6px solid #d9534f;background:#fafafa;padding:20px 22px;margin:26px 0;border-radius:8px">
<strong>⚠️ Higher leverage does not make the market more volatile</strong><br>
The underlying market movement is the same, but higher leverage generally leaves a smaller margin buffer. As a result, a relatively small adverse price movement may bring the position closer to liquidation more quickly.

<h2 style="font-size:27px;line-height:1.5">9. When Is Isolated Margin or Cross Margin Useful for Risk Management?</h2>

<h3 style="font-size:23px;line-height:1.6">Isolated Margin Focuses on Position-Level Risk Isolation</h3>

<p>If your goal is to limit the amount of margin available to a particular trading strategy, Isolated Margin provides a more direct structure.</p>

<p>For example:</p>

<ul>
<li>You use completely different strategies for different assets;</li>
<li>You do not want one bad position to keep consuming other funds;</li>
<li>You want each position to have a relatively clear margin limit.</li>
</ul>

<h3 style="font-size:23px;line-height:1.6">Cross Margin Focuses on Shared Margin and Capital Efficiency</h3>

<p>If you manage multiple positions in the same account, Cross Margin allows eligible assets and unrealized P&amp;L to participate in account-level risk calculations.</p>

<p>This can improve capital efficiency, but it also means:</p>

<p><strong>The risk of one position is no longer fully contained within that position and may gradually affect the entire Unified Trading Account.</strong></p>

<p>Therefore, a more distant reference Liquidation Price under Cross Margin should not automatically be interpreted as lower risk.</p>
